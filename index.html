Research project

The safe-fix gap

AI has made finding security flaws cheap. It hasn't made fixing them safely any cheaper, and attackers are chasing the same flaws. I'm trying to find out how big that gap really is.

Answer four questions

What I'm asking

If you run or lead security or IT, I'd love two minutes of your time. There are four questions on how long fixes take, how much of that time protects you, and how often fixes have to be redone. Rough answers are fine. "No idea" and "we don't track it" are useful answers too.

The four questions

It takes about two minutes and works on a phone.

Answer the questions

How your answers are used

  • Answers are anonymous. The form doesn't collect names or email addresses.
  • Everything is reported in aggregate, under the Chatham House Rule. I'll say "of the N who answered", and only give percentages once there are about 30 answers.
  • Background questions use broad bands (sector, size, role), and each one has a "prefer not to say" option.
  • Answers are stored in a Google Sheet that only I can access. Raw answers are deleted when the project ends, and by 31 December 2027 at the latest.
  • Questions, or want your answers removed? Contact me at safefixgap.research@gmail.com.

Where this is going

I publish the work in dated versions, so you can see how the argument changes as answers come in.
